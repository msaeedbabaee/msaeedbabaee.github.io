In geotechnical engineering, evaluating the stability of soil and rock slopes is one of the most critical responsibilities for ensuring the safety of infrastructure, mining, and civil engineering projects. To calculate the Factor of Safety ($\text\{FoS}$) and understand potential failure mechanisms, two primary and standardized approaches are utilized: the **Limit Equilibrium Method (LEM)** and the **Shear Strength Reduction (SSR)** method, which is based on the Finite Element Method ($\text\{FEM}$) or Finite Difference Method ($\text\{FDM}$).

\


This comprehensive article explores the theory, mathematical formulations, pros, cons, alternative nomenclature, project duration considerations (temporary vs. permanent), material behavior (soil vs. rock), search algorithms, and industrial applications of both methods.

\


## 1. Alternative Names

In international technical literature and design codes, these methods are referred to by several alternative names:

\


* **For Limit Equilibrium Method (LEM):**

  \


  * Method of Slices


  * Rigid-Plastic Slope Stability Analysis


  * Force and Moment Equilibrium Methods


* **For Shear Strength Reduction (SSR):**

  \


  * $c-\phi$ Reduction Technique


  * $\text\{FEM}$-based Slope Stability Analysis


  * Continuum Plasticity Analysis



## 2. Mathematical Formulations

### A. Limit Equilibrium Method (LEM) Formulation

In LEM, the Factor of Safety ($\text\{FoS}$) is defined as the ratio of total available shear strength ($\tau\_f$) to the mobilized shear stress required for equilibrium ($\tau$) along a slip surface:

\


$$\text\{FoS} = \frac\{\tau\_f}\{\tau} = \frac\{c' + \sigma\_n' \tan \phi'}\{\tau}$$

Based on the Mohr-Coulomb failure criterion, rigid-body static equilibrium equations are established for each slice of the sliding mass:

\


1. **Vertical Force Equilibrium ($\sum F\_y = 0$):**

   \


   $$W - N' \cos \alpha - T \sin \alpha - u L \cos \alpha = 0$$
2. **Horizontal Force Equilibrium ($\sum F\_x = 0$):**

   \


   $$T \cos \alpha - N' \sin \alpha - k\_h W = 0$$
3. **Moment Equilibrium about a Center of Rotation ($\sum M = 0$):**

   \


   $$\sum (W x - T R - N' f) = 0$$

Where $W$ is slice weight, $N'$ is effective normal force, $T$ is mobilized shear force, $\alpha$ is the base inclination angle, $u$ is pore water pressure, and $k\_h$ is the horizontal seismic acceleration coefficient. Different LEM variants (e.g., Bishop, Spencer, Morgenstern-Price) differ in how they satisfy interslice boundary forces.

\


### B. Shear Strength Reduction (SSR) Formulation

In the SSR method, the soil shear strength parameters ($c$ and $\tan \phi$) are systematically divided by a trial factor $F^\{trial}$ to obtain reduced strength parameters ($c\_\{trial}$ and $\phi\_\{trial}$):

\


$$c\_\{trial} = \frac\{c}\{F^\{trial}}, \quad \phi\_\{trial} = \arctan\left(\frac\{\tan \phi}\{F^\{trial}}\right)$$

The governing continuum mechanics equilibrium equations (based on virtual work or finite element stress equilibrium) are expressed as:

\


$$\frac\{\partial \sigma\_\{ij}}\{\partial x\_j} + \rho g\_i = 0$$

The computational procedure involves incrementally increasing $F^\{trial}$ until numerical convergence fails ($\mathbf\{K} \cdot \mathbf\{u} = \mathbf\{F}$ diverges). At this critical point, the system undergoes plastic collapse, and the value of $F^\{trial}$ at the moment of numerical divergence is reported as the final Factor of Safety ($\text\{FoS}$).

\


## 3. Slip Surface Search Algorithms

Because real-world slopes lack a predetermined exact failure path, numerical tools use optimization and search algorithms to locate the critical slip surface (minimum $\text\{FoS}$):

\


1. **Grid Search:** Establishing a grid of rotation centers and radii to locate the most critical circular arc (commonly used in traditional LEM).


2. **Block Search / Global Optimization (PSO / Cuckoo Search):** Advanced heuristic algorithms used to locate complex 3D and 2D non-circular failure surfaces.


3. **Genetic Algorithms (GA):** Inspired by natural genetics, capable of exploring vast spaces of compound, irregular slip surfaces.


4. **Gradient-based / Automatic Shear Banding:** In SSR, geometric surface searching is unnecessary because the failure path develops naturally where cumulative plastic shear strains reach their maximum.



## 4. Soil vs. Rock Behavior, Project Duration, and Design Life

The choice of analytical method and interpretation of results depend heavily on project-specific conditions:

\


* **Soil vs. Rock Masses:**

  \


  * **Soils:** Exhibit continuum or quasi-continuum behavior. Mohr-Coulomb or Hardening Soil constitutive models work exceptionally well within SSR. LEM is also widely used for homogeneous and stratified soils.


  * **Rock Masses:** Characterized by joints, faults, and discontinuities, requiring advanced failure criteria such as **Hoek-Brown**. In jointed rock masses, 3D limit equilibrium or Keyblock analysis is frequently employed.


* **Temporary Works:**

  \


  * Short-term urban excavations or temporary construction cuts. Acceptable safety factors are typically lower ($\text\{FoS} \approx 1.2 \text\{ to } 1.3$), and due to high processing speeds, LEM or rapid 2D analyses are preferred.


* **Permanent Structures & Long Design Life:**

  \


  * Large earth dams, tailings storage facilities, and major highway stabilization projects (with a design life of 50 to 100 years). These projects must account for weathering, long-term pore water pressure variations (phreatic line fluctuations), and creep. Consequently, comprehensive numerical analyses ($\text\{SSR / FEM}$) combined with coupled seepage and long-term deformation assessments are mandatory.



## 5. Comprehensive Comparison: Theory, Pros, and Cons

| **Comparison Criteria**           | **Limit Equilibrium Method (LEM)**                                 | **Shear Strength Reduction (SSR)**                                                     |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Alternative Names**             | Method of Slices, Rigid-Plastic, Force/Moment Equilibrium          | $c-\phi$ Reduction, Continuum Plasticity $\text\{FEM}$                                 |
| **Theoretical Foundation**        | Rigid-Body Statics                                                 | Continuum Mechanics & Stress-Strain Relations                                          |
| **Slip Surface Assumption**       | Mandatory (must pre-define or search circular/non-circular curves) | Unnecessary (failure surface develops automatically)                                   |
| **Deformation Calculation**       | None (calculates only $\text\{FoS}$ and slip location)             | Comprehensive (calculates exact settlements, lateral displacements, and plastic zones) |
| **Computational Speed**           | Extremely fast (seconds to minutes)                                | Time-consuming, requiring robust hardware                                              |
| **Material Behavior (Soil/Rock)** | Simple Mohr-Coulomb or basic anisotropy                            | Advanced rock models (Hoek-Brown) and overconsolidated soils                           |
| **Soil-Structure Interaction**    | Weak (anchors, piles, and nails modeled as external forces)        | Superior and realistic (full modeling of structural elements interacting with soil)    |

## 6. Industrial Applications

1. **LEM Applications:**

   \


   * Rapid stability control along long highway, railway, and water canal alignments.


   * Preliminary feasibility studies for open-pit mine benches and earthen cut slopes.


   * Projects lacking complex stress-strain data where standard shear parameters ($c$ and $\phi$) suffice.


2. **SSR Applications:**

   \


   * Design and analysis of deep urban excavations adjacent to sensitive structures (settlement control).


   * Large earth and rockfill dams requiring simultaneous transient seepage, pore pressure, and seismic stability evaluations.


   * High mining slopes and walls where progressive failure phenomena and non-linear deformations are critical.
