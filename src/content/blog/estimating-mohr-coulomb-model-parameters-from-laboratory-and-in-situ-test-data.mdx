Accurately determining Mohr-Coulomb parameters is a fundamental step in numerical modeling across general geotechnical software packages—such as PLAXIS, FLAC / FLAC3D, GEO5, SIGMA/W, RS2 / RS3, and MIDAS GTS NX. While advanced constitutive models capture stress-dependent stiffness and non-linear strain behavior, the Mohr-Coulomb (MC) linear elastic-perfectly plastic model remains the primary baseline across international consulting practices due to its transparency, computational efficiency, and widely accepted parameter relationships.

\


## 1. Applications, Advantages, and Limitations of the Mohr-Coulomb Model

### A) Typical Applications

* **Slope Stability & Landslides:** Calculating Factors of Safety ($FoS$) via shear strength reduction ($c\text\{-}\phi\text\{ reduction}$) techniques.


* **Foundation Bearing Capacity:** Estimating ultimate bearing capacity and settlement for shallow strip, isolated, or raft foundations.


* **Retaining Structures & Excavation Support:** Estimating active/passive earth pressures and global wall stability for anchored, soldier pile, or diaphragm walls.


* **Tunnelling & Caverns:** Preliminary stability assessment and plastic zone estimation in rock masses or stiff clays.



### B) Key Advantages

* **Simplicity & Minimum Parameters:** Requires only 5 physical parameters that are easily understood and obtainable from standard site investigations.


* **High Computational Efficiency:** Linear elasticity combined with a fixed yield surface ensures fast convergence in FEA/FDA solvers.


* **Direct Alignment with Design Codes:** Parameter inputs align directly with traditional limit equilibrium equations and standard design codes (AASHTO, Eurocode 7, etc.).



### C) Limitations

* **Constant Elastic Modulus:** Ignores stress-dependent stiffness (stiffness does not automatically increase with depth or confining stress).


* **Linear Elasticity Before Yield:** Overestimates soil stiffness at larger strain levels and fails to capture small-strain non-linearity ($G\_0$).


* **Symmetric Unloading/Reloading ($E\_\{ur} = E$):** Uses the same modulus for primary loading and unloading, leading to unrealistically large bottom heave predictions in deep excavations.


* **No Strain Hardening/Softening:** Assumes ideal plasticity, ignoring peak-to-residual strength drops in dense sands or overconsolidated clays.



## 2. Primary Mohr-Coulomb Model Parameters

Regardless of the software package used, the Mohr-Coulomb model relies on five core parameters:

\


| **Parameter**          | **Symbol**       | **Standard Units**         | **Engineering Role**                                        |
| ---------------------- | ---------------- | -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Young’s Modulus**    | $E$              | $\text\{kPa}, \text\{MPa}$ | Governs elastic stiffness and deformation response          |
| **Poisson’s Ratio**    | $\nu$            | dimensionless              | Controls lateral expansion and elastic volumetric response  |
| **Effective Cohesion** | $c'$ (or $s\_u$) | $\text\{kPa}$              | Baseline shear strength at zero normal stress               |
| **Friction Angle**     | $\phi'$          | degrees ($^\circ$)         | Confining stress-dependent shear strength gain              |
| **Dilatancy Angle**    | $\psi$           | degrees ($^\circ$)         | Controls plastic volumetric strain (expansion) during shear |

## 3. Laboratory Testing Procedures and Parameter Extraction

Laboratory testing—primarily Triaxial Compression (CD/CU/UU) and Direct Shear (DS) tests—provides the primary physical basis for parameter selection.

\


### A) Triaxial Compression Test (CD / CU)

#### 1. Testing Procedure & Data Recording

* **Specimen Preparation:** A cylindrical soil sample (typically $38\text\{ mm}$ or $50\text\{ mm}$ diameter with a $2:1$ height-to-diameter ratio) is saturated inside a triaxial cell.


* **Consolidation Phase:** Isotropic cell pressure ($\sigma\_3$) is applied. In a Consolidated Drained (CD) or Consolidated Undrained (CU) test, drainage is allowed until consolidation completes under $\sigma\_3$.


* **Shearing Phase:** Axial force ($F$) is applied continuously via a loading ram at a controlled strain rate while cell pressure ($\sigma\_3$) is kept constant.


* **Recorded Variables:**

  \


  * Axial Strain: $\varepsilon\_a = \frac\{\Delta L}\{L\_0}$


  * Deviator Stress: $q = \sigma\_1 - \sigma\_3 = \frac\{F}\{A\_c}$ (where $A\_c$ is the corrected cross-sectional area)


  * Volumetric Strain (CD test): $\varepsilon\_v = \frac\{\Delta V}\{V\_0}$


  * Excess Pore Water Pressure (CU test): $\Delta u$



#### 2. Parameter Extraction Equations

* **Secant Young’s Modulus ($E\_\{50}$):**

  \


  Since the Mohr-Coulomb model uses a single linear modulus, standard practice extracts the secant modulus at 50% of the peak deviator stress:



$$E\_\{50} = \frac\{0.5 \cdot q\_\{\max}}\{\varepsilon\_\{50}}$$

*(where $q\_\{\max} = \sigma\_\{1,f} - \sigma\_\{3,f}$, and $\varepsilon\_\{50}$ is the axial strain at $0.5 \cdot q\_\{\max}$)*

\


* **Effective Cohesion ($c'$) and Friction Angle ($\phi'$):**

  \


  1. Construct effective stress Mohr circles at failure using principal stresses:



$$\sigma'\_\{1,f} = \sigma\_\{1,f} - u\_f, \quad \sigma'\_\{3,f} = \sigma\_\{3,f} - u\_f$$

2. Draw the best-fit failure envelope tangent to the circles:



$$\tau\_f = c' + \sigma' \tan(\phi')$$

3. **Alternative ($p'\text\{-}q$ Space Formulation):** Plot $p' = \frac\{\sigma'\_1 + \sigma'\_3}\{2}$ against $q = \sigma'\_1 - \sigma'\_3$ at failure. The linear slope $M$ and intercept $d$ convert via:



$$\sin(\phi') = \frac\{M}\{2 + M}, \quad c' = d \cdot \frac\{1 - \sin(\phi')}\{2 \cdot \cos(\phi')}$$

* **Dilatancy Angle ($\psi$):**

  \


  Derived from the maximum rate of volume expansion relative to axial strain during shear using Hansen/Davis relations:



$$\sin(\psi) = \frac\{-\frac\{d\varepsilon\_v}\{d\varepsilon\_a}}\{2 - \frac\{d\varepsilon\_v}\{d\varepsilon\_a}}$$

*(Alternatively, via Bolton's empirical relation for dense sands: $\psi \approx \phi' - 30^\circ$)*

\


### B) Undrained Behavior Frameworks (Undrained A, B, and C)

When modeling low-permeability soils (clays and silts) under rapid loading, choosing the correct undrained strategy is critical:

\


1. **Undrained A (Effective Stress Approach):**

   \


   * Uses effective parameters ($c', \phi', E'$).


   * Calculates excess pore water pressure automatically via the bulk modulus of water ($K\_w$).


   * Requires effective Poisson's ratio ($\nu' \approx 0.20 - 0.30$).


2. **Undrained B (Total Stress Approach with $s\_u$):**

   \


   * Uses undrained shear strength ($s\_u$) with $\phi\_u = 0^\circ$.


   * Stiffness is defined using undrained Young's modulus ($E\_u$).


3. **Undrained C (Pure Total Stress):**

   \


   * All parameters are total stress parameters ($c = s\_u, \phi = 0^\circ, E = E\_u, \nu = 0.495$). No excess pore water pressures are generated.



> **Modulus Conversion:** Undrained Young's Modulus ($E\_u$) relates to Effective Young's Modulus ($E'$) via:
>
>

$$E\_u = E' \cdot \frac\{2(1 + \nu\_u)}\{2(1 + \nu')} \approx 1.25 \\, E' \quad (\text\{for } \nu' = 0.20, \nu\_u = 0.495)$$

## 4. In-Situ Field Test Correlations

When obtaining undisturbed samples is difficult, field tests offer vital parameter estimates.

\


### A) Standard Penetration Test (SPT)

Using energy- and overburden-corrected blow counts ($N\_\{60}$ or $(N\_1)\_\{60}$):

\


* **Friction Angle ($\phi'$):**



$$\phi' \approx 27.1 + 0.3 \cdot (N\_1)\_\{60} - 0.00054 \cdot \left\[(N\_1)\_\{60}\right]^2 \quad \text\{(Peck, Hanson, \\& Thornburn)}$$

* **Young’s Modulus ($E$):**



$$\text\{NC Sands: } E \\, (\text\{MPa}) = 0.75 \cdot N\_\{60}$$

$$\text\{Dense Sands / Gravels: } E \\, (\text\{MPa}) = (1.0 \text\{ to } 1.2) \cdot N\_\{60}$$

### B) Cone Penetration Test (CPT)

* **Friction Angle ($\phi'$):**



$$\phi' = \arctan \left\[ 0.1 + 0.38 \cdot \log\left(\frac\{q\_c}\{\sigma'\_\{v0}}\right) \right]$$

* **Undrained Shear Strength ($s\_u$):**



$$s\_u = \frac\{q\_c - \sigma\_\{v0}}\{N\_\{kt}} \quad (N\_\{kt} = 12 - 18)$$

* **Young’s Modulus ($E$):**



$$E = 2.5 \cdot q\_c \quad (\text\{Sands}), \quad E = (3 \text\{ to } 8) \cdot q\_c \quad (\text\{Clays})$$

### C) Pressuremeter Test (PMT) & Dilatometer Test (DMT)

* **PMT:** Directly provides the pressuremeter modulus ($E\_\{\text\{PMT}}$) and reload modulus ($E\_\{ur}$), which are highly reliable for foundation design without sample disturbance effects.


* **DMT:** Provides the dilatometer modulus ($E\_D$) and constrained modulus ($M\_\{\text\{DMT}}$), allowing accurate evaluation of stress history ($OCR$) and initial $K\_0$.

## 6. Worked Numerical Example (Laboratory Extraction)

### Scenario

A Drained Triaxial Test (CD) on a dense sand sample ($\sigma'\_3 = 100\text\{ kPa}$) yields:

\


* Peak deviator stress: $q\_\{\max} = 350\text\{ kPa}$ at axial strain $\varepsilon\_a = 4.2\\%$.


* At $50\\%$ peak deviator stress ($q\_\{50} = 175\text\{ kPa}$), axial strain is $\varepsilon\_\{50} = 0.50\\%$ ($0.0050$).


* Rate of volumetric expansion: $\frac\{d\varepsilon\_v}\{d\varepsilon\_a} = -0.18$.



### Calculation Steps

1. **Secant Modulus ($E\_\{50}$):**



$$E\_\{50} = \frac\{175\text\{ kPa}}\{0.0050} = 35,000\text\{ kPa} = 35\text\{ MPa}$$

2. **Major Principal Stress at Failure ($\sigma'\_\{1,f}$):**



$$\sigma'\_\{1,f} = q\_\{\max} + \sigma'\_3 = 350 + 100 = 450\text\{ kPa}$$

3. **Friction Angle ($\phi'$)** *(assuming $c' \approx 0$ for numerical evaluation)*:



$$\sin(\phi') = \frac\{\sigma'\_\{1,f} - \sigma'\_\{3,f}}\{\sigma'\_\{1,f} + \sigma'\_\{3,f}} = \frac\{450 - 100}\{450 + 100} = \frac\{350}\{550} = 0.636 \implies \phi' = 39.5^\circ$$

4. **Dilatancy Angle ($\psi$):**



$$\sin(\psi) = \frac\{-(-0.18)}\{2 - (-0.18)} = \frac\{0.18}\{2.18} = 0.0825 \implies \psi \approx 4.7^\circ$$

*(Using Bolton's empirical estimate: $\psi \approx \phi' - 30^\circ = 39.5^\circ - 30^\circ = 9.5^\circ$)*

\


## 7. Common Pitfalls and Modeling Errors

1. **Setting Cohesion to Absolute Zero ($c' = 0$):**

   \


   * *Issue:* Purely frictional materials with $c' = 0$ cause division-by-zero or numerical singularities at stress-free ground surfaces in FEA software (PLAXIS / RS2).


   * *Fix:* Always apply a small artificial cohesion ($c' = 1 - 2\text\{ kPa}$).


2. **Inputting $\nu = 0.50$ for Undrained Coupled Analysis:**

   \


   * *Issue:* Entering $\nu = 0.50$ in effective stress coupled undrained formulations (PLAXIS Undrained A) leads to an infinite bulk modulus ($K \to \infty$) and immediate solver crashes.


   * *Fix:* Input drained Poisson’s ratio ($\nu' \approx 0.20 - 0.30$). The software handles water stiffness automatically via the fluid bulk modulus.


3. **Uncontrolled Volumetric Expansion (Missing Dilatancy Cut-off):**

   \


   * *Issue:* A non-zero dilatancy angle ($\psi > 0$) without a maximum void ratio limit causes soil elements to expand infinitely under plastic shear strain.


   * *Fix:* Activate **Dilatancy Cut-off** in software settings by specifying initial, minimum, and maximum void ratios ($e\_\{\min}, e\_\{\max}$).


4. **Ignoring Tension Cut-off Settings:**

   \


   * *Issue:* Soil elements near excavations or slope crests may develop unrealistically high tensile stresses.


   * *Fix:* Enable **Tension Cut-off** and set allowable tensile strength ($\sigma\_t \le c' \cot \phi'$ or $\sigma\_t = 0$).
